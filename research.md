# Gain App — Research (Chris Braxton)
_Oct 14 2026. Chain/brand numbers re-verified against official pages (see app Me → Sources); app computes 3,100 kcal / 155 g protein for Chris._ _ Nutrition/menu figures are approximate; check them against the current chain nutrition calculators before putting them in the app._

## Profile
Male, 37, 6'0" (182.9 cm), 174 lb (78.9 kg). Goal is 190 lb (+16 lb). He's a hard gainer who lifted for 1 year, took 6 months off, and is restarting now. No injuries. He drives daily to Upland, Torrance, and Hawthorne. Dinner (7–9 PM, cooked by his girlfriend) is his only steady meal.

## 1. Targets (math)
- BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor): 10×78.9 + 6.25×182.9 − 5×37 + 5 = **~1,752 kcal**
- TDEE at ×1.55 (lifting 3–4×/wk plus a desk/car job): **~2,700 kcal**
- Lean-bulk surplus of +300–500 kcal: **start at 3,100 kcal/day**. Hard gainers often underestimate what they burn (NEAT), so the app should adjust this from his real trend.
- Protein at 1.6–2.2 g/kg (ISSN; Morton 2018 meta): 126–174 g. **Target 160 g/day** (~0.9 g/lb)
- Fat ~25–30% of calories (≈90–100 g). Carbs fill the rest (≈400 g)
- Gain rate: 0.25–0.5% of body weight per week (≈0.5–0.75 lb/wk) for trained/returning lifters (Iraki et al. 2019). At that pace, 16 lb takes **~5–8 months**. He may gain faster in the first 2–4 weeks from glycogen/water plus muscle memory.
- Auto-adjust: look at a 2-week trend of the 7-day average weight. If he's gaining < 0.25 lb/wk, add 150–200 kcal. If > 1 lb/wk after the first month, subtract 100–150 kcal (MacroFactor-style).
- Sleep 7–9 h. Restricting sleep reduces muscle gain and raises the share of fat in weight gained (Nedeltcheva 2010; Dattilo 2011).

## 2. Training (returning lifter)
- Volume: ~10–20 hard sets per muscle per week. **Restart at ~8–10 sets** for weeks 1–2 and build up. Muscle memory brings strength back quickly (Bruusgaard 2010; Schoenfeld 2017 volume meta).
- Reps: 6–12 on compounds and 10–20 on isolation, stopping 1–3 reps short of failure (RIR 1–3). Load works across a wide rep range (Schoenfeld 2017).
- Progression: double progression. When all sets hit the top of the rep range, add 5 lb (upper body) or 10 lb (lower body).
- Deload every 5–8 weeks, or when performance stalls 2 sessions in a row: about half the sets, same weights.
- Keep cardio low (1–2 short sessions). Count kickboxing classes as calories burned; each one costs roughly +300–500 kcal.

**Gym: UFC Gym Alhambra** (412 E Main St, open 24h). Equipment: free weights, strength machines, Olympic lifting platforms, turf, cardio, bag area, MMA/BJJ rooms, sauna/hot tub/pool, and recovery. Classes: kickboxing conditioning (Champion/Ultimate tiers), MMA, BJJ, boxing, cycling, yoga, and group fitness.

**Suggested split: Upper/Lower × 2 at the gym, plus 1 optional home dumbbell day**
- Mon Upper A: bench 3×6–8, cable/DB row 3×8–10, OHP 2×8, lat pulldown 3×10, curls/triceps 2×12
- Tue Lower A: squat 3×6–8, RDL 3×8, leg press 2×12, leg curl 2×12, calves
- Thu Upper B: incline DB press, pull-ups/assisted, seated row, lateral raises, curls/triceps
- Fri Lower B: deadlift or trap bar 3×5, Bulgarian split squat, leg extension, leg curl, abs
- Sat/home (5–25 lb DBs): high-rep or tempo work. Goblet squat, DB floor press, one-arm rows, lateral raises, curls, push-ups to RIR 1–2. Also good for days he can't make the gym.

## 4. Competitor apps: loved features and complaints
- **MacroFactor**: people love the adaptive TDEE/weight trend algorithm, fast logging, and that it doesn't shame you. Complaint: no free tier.
- **RP Hypertrophy**: people love the auto volume/RIR progression with feedback (pump, soreness) and the mesocycles/deloads. Complaints: price, and it feels rigid.
- **Strong**: people love the clean, fast logger, rest timer, and PR tracking. Complaint: the free tier only allows 3 routines.
- **Hevy**: people love the generous free tier, social feed, and PR charts. Complaint: no smart programming.
- **Fitbod**: people love that it auto-generates workouts around the equipment you have. Complaints: odd exercise picks, and progression is hard to control.
- **Boostcamp**: people love the free, proven programs (GZCL, PPL, nSuns). Complaint: limited customization.
- **Cronometer**: people love the accurate, verified micronutrient data. Complaint: the UI feels clunky.
- **MyFitnessPal**: people love the huge food database (chains included) and barcode scanning. Complaints: errors from user-submitted entries, ads, and barcode scanning moved behind the paywall.
- **Lose It**: people love the simple UI and streaks. Complaint: it's built around weight loss.

Common complaints across all of them: logging friction, paywalls, nutrition and training living in separate apps, and no bulking-specific coaching.

## 5. Recommended features
1. Onboarding wizard: stats, goal, and activity, then it computes calories, protein, and gain rate (the math above, with an editable surplus). Each profile has its own goal (gain, maintain, or lose) so his girlfriend can set up hers.
2. Meal schedule template built around his day (the table above), with check-off "slots" instead of gram-level logging.
3. Quick log: one tap for saved favorites (the shake, Chipotle bowl, Double-Double), plus a chain pick list filtered by "near Torrance/Upland/Hawthorne". Optional custom entry with kcal and protein.
4. Reminders (PWA notifications) at each slot, plus a "you're 1,200 kcal behind at 4 PM — here's a fix" nudge.
5. Weigh-ins (daily or weekly), a 7-day average trend chart, and weekly auto calorie adjustment.
6. Workout program: upper/lower plus a home dumbbell day, with last session's weights shown, double-progression suggestions, a rest timer, a deload prompt, and an equipment toggle (gym vs. home 5–25 lb).
7. Progressive overload charts: estimated 1RM, volume per muscle per week.
8. Milestones and achievements: every +2 lb, protein streaks, PRs, workout streaks.
9. Progress photos (Supabase storage, private) with side-by-side compare.
10. Multiple profiles under a family code (same pattern as the Isaiah app). Offline-first PWA on GitHub Pages with Supabase sync.
11. Sleep log (hours) and a weekly check-in (energy, hunger, soreness).
